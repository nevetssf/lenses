# Leica Summilux f/1.4 (Pre-ASPH) v1

*2026-08-13*

My copy arrived with serious internal haze and traces of oil on the blades. This is a common occurrence with vintage lense from the 1950's and 60's. The oil they used tended to evaporate and leave deposits on the surfaces closest to the blades, and migrate around, showing up most clearly on the blades.

Fortunately, there are some good resources online, and the lense is relatively straightforward to service.

The optical block unscrews from the main body.

## Re-painting the lettering

The black and red distnce scales were missing a lot of paint, so I cleaned them with acetone using a cotton swab and repainted them using Testors Enamel Model Paint. The procedure is documented elsewhere and everyone seems to have a slightly different approach, so I'll just outline what I did.

- Used Gloss Dark Bright Red and and Gloss Black for the lettering. There is a regular Gloss Red, but it was a bit too orange. Stir the paint well with a toothpick or other small implement.
- After cleaning the existing lettering with a q-tip, apply a tiny bit of paint to the lettering, just enough to fill in the grooves. Let it sit for minute or two, then use a Kimwipe folded over a few times to wipe off the excess, across the lettering. The first wipe will be a bit of a mess. Don't press too hard or you'll remove the recessed paint. The put a drop of 99.9% Isopropyl on a clean section of the folded kimwipe and do a few passes to get rid of the remaining excess paint. You'll get a feel for how much pressure to apply with practice. A Kimwipe is useful becuase it's thin and doesn't shred or leave lint. 
- Proceed in sets of 2-3 letters or words.

It'll take a few days for the lettering to fully cure, but you can handle it (carefully) right away.
