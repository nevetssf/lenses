# Leica Summicron 35mm f/2 (Version 1, "8-element")

The first Summicron 35mm f/2, produced from 1958 to 1969, is one of the most sought-after lenses in the Leica M lineup. Its 8-element/6-group double-Gauss design was created by Walter Mandler at Leitz Canada and is widely considered a benchmark for wide-angle rendering — sharp across the frame even wide open, with smooth tonal transitions and a signature three-dimensional look that later versions never quite replicated.

Produced in both M mount (from 1958) and Leica Thread Mount (LTM/M39, from 1959), with a small run of "goggled" versions equipped with an optical adapter for use on the M3. Original examples are collectible and have appreciated substantially; a clean, hazeless copy commands a premium.

Common service issues are haze on internal elements (particularly the cemented groups), stiff or dried helicoid grease, and oil migration onto aperture blades. The 8-element construction means more air-to-glass surfaces to clean, and the cemented groups are original Canada balsam or early synthetic cement — separation is possible on the oldest examples.
